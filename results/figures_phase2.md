## Figure 4 — deleting kernels, not making the math faster

`results/fig4_phase2_waterfall.png` / `.pdf`

Waterfall from the HF CUDA graph to the fused Triton step at 128 tokens.
Every term is a measured delta; the last two bars are measurement accounting
(CUPTI inflates kernel durations, and the HF step contains a host gap).

| step | ms |
|---|---:|
| HF graph (wall) | 3.360 |
| host gap removed | -0.256 |
| 1,100 elementwise kernels removed | -1.241 |
| attention | +0.027 |
| sampling + memcpy | +0.003 |
| weight GEMVs | -0.004 |
| fused (kernel sum) | 1.889 |
| CUPTI inflation | -0.194 |
| **fused (wall)** | **1.695** |

The point of the figure is the weight row: HF's pure GEMM total is 1.5946 ms and
the fused GEMVs, which now also do the norm scaling, residual add, SiLU and
next-norm partial sums, are 1.5906 ms. The 2x came from deleting 1,118 launches
and the host gap, not from a faster weight stream.

Source: `results/breakdown_graph_128.json`, `results/breakdown_graph_fused_128.json`.

## Figure 5 — where the 0.57 ms above the weight floor lives

`results/fig5_projection_gap.png` / `.pdf`

Each weight kernel group split into "bytes / 263.5 GB/s" (solid) and
"measured - floor" (hatched). Also written to
`results/phase2_per_projection.csv`.

| projection | MB | floor | measured | % of floor | CTAs | overhead/kernel |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| gate+up | 106.17 | 0.403 | 0.585 | 69% | 48 | 6.1 µs |
| down | 53.08 | 0.201 | 0.321 | 63% | 18 | 4.0 µs |
| lm_head | 56.62 | 0.215 | 0.291 | 74% | 1 cutlass | 76 µs total |
| qkv | 33.18 | 0.126 | 0.219 | 57% | 30 | 3.1 µs |
| o_proj | 19.91 | 0.076 | 0.174 | **44%** | **18** | 3.3 µs |
| **total** | 268.96 | 1.021 | 1.590 | **64%** | 121 | ~4 µs |

Two mechanisms explain the gap:

- **`o_proj` launches 18 CTAs on 24 SMs** — 0.75 of a wave, six SMs idle for the
  whole kernel, with only 37 KB per CTA to amortise the ramp.
- **Every kernel pays 3-6 µs above its bytes**; 121 kernels x ~4 µs is 0.49 of
  the 0.57 ms gap. `gate+up` pays double because 48 CTAs is exactly two waves.

This is the figure that motivates Phase 3: the loss is per-kernel ramp/tail and
wave quantization, which a persistent kernel removes.

Source: `results/trace_breakdown_graph_fused_128.json` (per-kernel durations),
weight shapes from the model config.

## Figure 6 — context scaling at batch 1

`results/fig6_context_scaling.png` / `.pdf`

Left: ms/token vs prompt length. Right: percent of the 263.5 GB/s roofline,
weight-only vs weight+KV.

| prompt | fused | HF graph | vLLM | llama.cpp F16+FA | llama.cpp Q4_K_M |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 128 | **1.711** | 3.391 | 2.296 | 2.322 | 1.750 |
| 512 | **1.726** | 3.429 | 2.184 | 2.367 | 1.699 |
| 2048 | **2.003** | 3.715 | 2.414 | 2.543 | 1.884 |

| prompt | fused weight % | fused total % | HF total % |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 128 | 59.7 | 60.5 | 30.5 |
| 512 | 59.2 | 61.9 | 31.2 |
| 2048 | 51.0 | 60.0 | 32.4 |

The weight-only percentage falls at 2048 only because the denominator ignores
the growing KV term; total-traffic efficiency is flat at ~60%. Attention goes
0.196 -> 0.505 ms (12% -> 26% of the step) and accounts for the whole 0.28 ms
increase from 512 to 2048.

The 4-bit llama.cpp line is included on purpose: at 0.104 GB it matches or beats
the bf16 fused path at 512/2048. Beating that at bf16 requires cutting bytes,
which is out of Phase 3 scope.

Source: `results/phase2_verify.json` (fused), `results/phase1.csv` (HF graph,
vLLM), `results/llamacpp.csv` (F16+FA and Q4_K_M, flash-attn on).

## Figure 7 — one decode step, drawn to scale

`results/fig7_step_timeline.png` / `.pdf`

Top two rows: every kernel in one decode step as a bar at its real timestamp,
coloured by category. Bottom: how kernel time splits across SM-coverage bands,
where coverage is `min(blocks, 24) / 24` per kernel.

| mode | kernels | kernel span | <25% | 25-50% | 50-75% | 75-100% |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| HF graph | 1,372 | 3.16 ms | **40%** | 1% | 0% | 58% |
| fused | 254 | 1.90 ms | 3% | 2% | 2% | **92%** |

The HF step is a comb of ~1,100 sub-2 µs launches, most of which put 1-3 blocks
on the GPU; 40% of its kernel time runs with less than a quarter of the SMs
occupied. The fused step is 121 wide weight bands and never spends meaningful
time below 50%.

Raw per-kernel data: `results/phase2_step_timeline.csv` (1,626 rows).

Source: `results/trace_breakdown_graph_128.json`,
`results/trace_breakdown_graph_fused_128.json` (5-8 profiled steps; the last
full step is used).

## Figure 8 — B=1 and B=8 are different problems

`results/fig8_batch_plane.png` / `.pdf`

Per-request latency vs aggregate decode throughput, B = 1/2/4/8, at 128 and 2048
tokens.

| B | HF graph 128 | vLLM 128 | HF graph 2048 | vLLM 2048 |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 318 tok/s @ 3.15 ms | 350 @ 2.86 | 314 @ 3.19 | 366 @ 2.73 |
| 2 | 551 @ 3.63 | 784 @ 2.55 | 486 @ 4.11 | 651 @ 3.07 |
| 4 | 1075 @ 3.72 | 1601 @ 2.50 | 879 @ 4.55 | 1218 @ 3.29 |
| 8 | 2139 @ 3.74 | **2954 @ 2.71** | 1468 @ 5.45 | **2051 @ 3.90** |

The fused batch-1 point is off the vLLM curve at both lengths: ~585 tok/s at
1.71 ms (128) and 499 tok/s at 2.00 ms (2048). At B=8 vLLM is 5.0x the fused
aggregate throughput at 128 and 4.1x at 2048.

The dashed line is the batch-1 weight floor; the ceiling annotation is the
weight-only bound `979 x B` tok/s (weights read once per step, one token per
request per step). Real batching lands far below it because KV and attention
traffic grow with B — which is why B=1 and B=8 are not the same optimisation.

Source: `results/batch_hf_graph.json`, `results/batch_vllm.json`, fused point
from `results/phase2_verify.json`. vLLM used `max_model_len=2114` for the whole
sweep, so its B=1 number differs from the Part 1 per-prompt runs.

## Figure 9 — fusion is free only when it does not move the reduction

`results/fig9_norm_design.png` / `.pdf`

The qkv stage (30 layers, cold-cache microbenchmark with a 256 MB read-only
flush) under four placements of the RMSNorm reduction. Also written to
`results/phase2_design_space.csv`.

| design | ms | delta |
|---|---:|---:|
| pure GEMV, no norm | 0.212 | — |
| 1-CTA `norm_stats` + GEMV (extra launch) | 0.258 | +46 µs |
| norm reduction fused into every block | 0.312 | **+100 µs** |
| producer partials + GEMV (shipped) | 0.219 | +7 µs |

Fusing the reduction into every consumer serialises the weight stream: each
block waits for the full 576-element sum before it can start streaming rows.
Fusing it into the *producer* (each block writes `sum(y_bf16^2)` for the next
layer) costs 7 µs for 30 layers. The tempting design is slower than the three
kernels it replaces.

Source: microbenchmark numbers recorded in
`results/phase2_notebook.md` §"RMSNorm reduction placement".

## Using them in the writeup

1. **Figure 7** opens: decode is a launch-count and occupancy problem.
2. **Figure 9** states the rule: a boundary is a reduction boundary.
3. **Figure 4** is the result: 2x by deleting kernels.
4. **Figure 5** is the diagnosis: 64% of the floor, lost to ramp and waves.
5. **Figure 6** bounds the context story.
6. **Figure 8** bounds the batch story and justifies staying at B=1.

## Caveats

- Figures 4, 5 and 7 use profiled kernel durations. CUPTI inflates them ~10%
  under WSL, and the fused step's kernel sum (1.889 ms) exceeds its wall time
  (1.695 ms). Wall times are the headline; kernel times are for attribution.
- Figure 6 uses the `phase2_verify` sweep, so the fused point is 1.711 ms rather
  than the 1.695 ms dedicated run (0.9% difference). The HF graph is
  clock-sensitive: Part 1 canonical values are 3.39 / 3.43 / 3.72 ms.
- Figure 8 mixes the batch sweep (HF/vLLM) with the main harness (fused). The
  vLLM `max_model_len` caveat above applies; use within-sweep trends.
- Figure 7's coverage metric is per-kernel SM occupancy, not sustained bandwidth
  utilisation. It ignores per-CTA warp count and memory-level parallelism, so
  treat it as a diagnostic, not a roofline substitute.
